# Example — Static Electricity at the Laundromat

## Why this example is here

This is a **successful historical example** from the development of this skill.

Important accuracy note:

The image-generation call did **not** receive a separately preserved verbatim prompt. The image tool inferred its instructions from the conversation context. Therefore, this file records the **closest recoverable generation brief**: the page plan, narrative decisions, and medium constraints that immediately preceded the successful result.

Do not treat this as a prompt template to copy mechanically. Study the transformation from a small premise into a readable page.

Output image:

`static-electricity-laundromat.png`

---

# 1. Starting request

The user wanted:

- a true black-and-white screentone manga page
- a small story rather than a static illustration
- paneling that serves narrative importance
- empty / quiet panels when useful
- backgrounds that may reduce to contours and tone rather than fully rendered realism
- a beginning–development–turn–landing rhythm
- the two established characters preserved without using a character reference image for this generation

The assistant chose a small late-night laundromat story.

---

# 2. Page concept

Title used internally:

**Static Electricity**

Location:

**Late-night self-service laundromat**

Narrative engine:

**missing sock → discovery → static-electricity gag → accidental hand contact → quiet romantic aftertaste**

The event is deliberately tiny. The page works because the object, reactions, panel hierarchy, and relationship shift all support one another.

---

# 3. Page-level story

A girl is searching through warm laundry because one sock is missing.

She notices someone nearby.

A tall young man appears holding the missing sock. It had stuck to his back because of static electricity.

The sock snaps back against him.

They both reach for it and their hands touch.

She says there is electricity.

He says it is static electricity.

In the final beat, she still refuses to take the sock because “it has electricity,” so he quietly keeps it.

The page lands on a tiny shift in intimacy rather than a confession.

---

# 4. Panel design

## Panel 1 — Quiet establishing shot

A wide, shallow horizontal panel.

Show the self-service laundromat late at night:
- glass door
- several circular washer / dryer doors
- simple tile or floor perspective
- darkness outside
- minimal signage / machine geometry

No characters.

The environment should be readable but not photorealistically rendered.

Narrative function:

**orientation + silence + breathing room**

The page begins quietly before introducing the problem.

---

## Panel 2 — Small practical problem

Medium-small panel.

The girl crouches beside the dryer and searches through the laundry.

She holds one sock and realizes the other is missing.

Her floppy dog ears and tail help the body language, but they are not the joke by themselves.

Background can be reduced because Panel 1 already established the room.

Narrative function:

**introduce the page-sized problem**

---

## Panel 3 — Attention shift

A narrow, small reaction panel.

Close-up of the girl's eye or face as she notices someone.

Use almost no background.

A small question mark is enough.

Narrative function:

**redirect attention**

The panel is intentionally visually sparse.

---

## Panel 4 — Character entrance

A larger panel.

The man enters the visual field holding the missing sock.

His dark shirt and dark hair should form a strong black mass against the white page.

His figure may push against or partly break the normal panel boundary if needed for presence.

He asks, in very short dialogue:

“这个？”

Narrative function:

**entrance + reveal**

The panel is larger because his entrance changes the situation.

---

## Panel 5 — Dry comedy beat

Small panel.

The girl looks at the sock.

The man looks at the sock.

The sock snaps back against his shirt because of static electricity.

Use a small sound effect / impact mark.

Keep the background nearly blank.

Narrative function:

**turn / visual gag**

The comedy should be dry rather than exaggerated into spectacle.

---

## Panel 6 — Hand close-up

Medium panel or narrow detail panel.

Both reach for the sock at the same time.

Their hands touch.

Do not turn this into a glossy romantic illustration.

The hands and the tiny contact are the whole point of the panel.

Dialogue rhythm:

Girl:
“……有电。”

Man:
“静电。”

Girl:
“我知道。”

Narrative function:

**convert the practical joke into a relationship beat**

---

## Panel 7 — Large landing panel

The largest panel, placed at the bottom.

The dryer remains open. Warm clothes form a foreground anchor.

The girl sits or crouches nearby in oversized warm clothing, hands partly hidden in sleeves.

The man is beside her holding or pocketing the recovered sock.

Dialogue:

Man:
“不要了？”

Girl:
“有电。”

He keeps the sock.

Leave a meaningful amount of open space around or between them rather than filling the entire panel with effects.

Narrative function:

**quiet payoff + changed relationship distance**

The ending should feel slightly warmer than the beginning.

---

# 5. Black-and-white decisions

The successful direction depended on treating black-and-white manga as a **material system**, not a grayscale filter.

Use:

- white paper
- solid black ink
- visible screentone
- line hatching where useful
- strong black masses for dark hair and clothing
- simplified background contours
- white / tone / black as distinct compositional values

Skin:

- mostly white
- minimal tone
- no soft digital volume shading

Hair:

- grouped ink structure
- strong dark mass
- selective white highlights
- avoid rendering every strand

Background:

- enough detail to establish the laundromat
- progressively lighter / simpler in reaction and contact panels
- no cinematic blur or airbrushed depth

# 6. Why the page worked

## Small event, real shift

Nothing “major” happens.

But the page changes from:
- girl alone with a missing sock

to:
- both characters sharing a private joke and a tiny physical / emotional contact

That is enough.

## Panel importance matched story importance

- opening environment: wide but shallow
- reaction: small
- entrance: larger
- visual gag: small
- hand contact: focused detail
- ending: largest

The panel hierarchy follows the narrative hierarchy.

## Background density changed with attention

The laundromat is established once.

When attention narrows to:
- a face
- the sock
- the hands

the background retreats.

This makes the page easier to read and keeps the emotional center clear.

## The object carries causality

The sock is not decoration.

It creates:
- the problem
- the reveal
- the gag
- the hand contact
- the ending

One object binds the whole page.

## The romantic beat grows out of practical action

The characters do not suddenly pose romantically.

The hand contact emerges naturally from both reaching for the same sock.

This is much stronger than adding romance as decoration.

---

# 7. What this example teaches the skill

When the user gives a simple character setup, the assistant can generate a satisfying manga page by finding:

1. one scene-native object
2. one immediate practical intention
3. one small friction
4. one character-specific reaction chain
5. one visual turn
6. one tiny relationship or emotional shift

Then panel size, background density, black/white structure, and close-up selection should all follow those beats.

This example should be read as:

**simple premise → internal page design → direct image generation**

not:

**user must write a long prompt**
