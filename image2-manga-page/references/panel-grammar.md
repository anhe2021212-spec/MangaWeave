# Panel Grammar

## Principle

Panel layout exists to control:
- reading order
- importance
- duration
- attention
- emotional pressure
- surprise
- pause

Do not average panel sizes for neatness.

Do not use irregular geometry as decoration.

## Common panel functions

### Establishing panel
Purpose:
- locate the reader
- define where characters are
- establish spatial relationships

Often:
- wide
- medium or small
- more background information than reaction panels

### Reaction panel
Purpose:
- show what changed internally
- delay response
- create comedy or emotional echo

Often:
- small
- face or body-language focused
- simplified background

### Narrow vertical panel
Purpose:
- gaze
- hand
- fingertips
- object
- physical distance
- elongated pause

### Large panel
Purpose:
- page-level payoff
- major emotional landing
- visual punchline
- reveal
- important character presence

### Diagonal / irregular panel
Purpose:
- emotional imbalance
- sudden attention shift
- subjective instability
- intensified intimacy

Use selectively.

### Border-breaking figure
Purpose:
- impactful entrance
- newly heightened awareness of a character
- moment when presence itself matters

Not a default “cool” effect.

### Silent / empty panel
Purpose:
- breathe
- process
- hesitate
- delay a joke
- create aftertaste

### Object close-up
Purpose:
- allow a prop to carry the story beat
- turn a tiny event into readable causality

## Panel hierarchy

Important beats get:
- more area
- more isolation
- stronger contrast
- more visual clarity

Minor beats can be:
- small
- repetitive
- plain
- background-light

Not every panel should look like a hero illustration.

## Repeated framing

Repeated or nearly repeated camera positions are useful when:
- a facial expression changes
- a joke lands after a delay
- body posture changes
- the reader should compare “before” and “after”

This is especially valuable in everyday manga.

## Spatial clarity

Before heavily simplifying backgrounds, first establish:
- who is where
- what object matters
- which direction people are facing
- how they can physically interact

Minimal background should feel intentional, not like missing information.
