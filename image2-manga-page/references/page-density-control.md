# Page Density Control

## Purpose

Prevent two opposite failures:

1. every panel is overloaded and equally loud
2. every panel becomes evenly sparse and equally flat

Breathing does **not** mean uniform emptiness.

Breathing means **contrast**:
- large vs small
- dense vs quiet
- framed vs open
- descriptive vs selective
- action vs pause

## Start from hierarchy, not count

Do not begin with:
- “make 5 panels”
- “make a balanced grid”
- “keep all panels equally clean”

Begin with panel roles.

Recommended role set for one page:
- **1 dominant panel**
- **1–2 support panels**
- **1–2 micro / pause / insert panels**
- **1 landing / aftertaste panel**

These may overlap, but the page should not feel evenly weighted.

## Dominant panel

A page should usually have one most important panel.

Its function may be:
- emotional landing
- strongest reaction
- character entrance
- relationship shift
- key reveal
- visual punchline

A dominant panel may be:
- the largest panel
- the most isolated panel
- an open panel with very little detail
- a border-breaking moment

Important does not mean busy.

A dominant panel can be one of the simplest panels on the page.

## Support panels

Used to:
- establish situation
- move action forward
- compare before/after
- prepare the dominant panel

These should not compete equally with the dominant panel.

## Micro / insert / pause panels

Use for:
- eye
- hand
- object
- tiny reaction
- silence
- hesitation
- time-stretch
- comedic beat

These are essential for rhythm.

Do not remove them just to make the page tidier.

## Breathing rules

At least one panel per page should mainly serve breathing, pause, or aftertaste.

Breathing may come from:
- white space
- simplified background
- a narrow vertical panel
- an object close-up
- a silent reaction panel
- an open landing panel

Do not interpret “breathing” as “all panels are airy.”

## Background-density distribution

Default suggestion:
- 1 panel may carry most of the environmental explanation
- 1–2 panels may include partial spatial support
- several other panels may reduce to minimal contour, tone, or white space

Do not fully render every environment in every panel.

## Mode-specific tendency

### Slice-of-life A
- more stable rectangles
- hierarchy can be milder
- density contrast mainly through reaction vs establish vs landing
- repeated framing is welcome

### Shoujo A
- stronger asymmetry is encouraged
- hierarchy should be more visibly uneven
- include at least one clear hierarchy change through one or more of:
  - very large panel
  - narrow vertical insert
  - diagonal cut
  - open panel
  - border-breaking figure
  - isolated local close-up

Shoujo pages should not read like a calm evenly spaced table.

## Anti-average rule

If every panel looks equally important, equally sparse, and similarly shaped, the page has failed hierarchy even if it has beautiful breathing room.

## Simple silhouette test

Imagine the page only as blocks.

If it resembles a calm spreadsheet, hierarchy is too weak.

If it has a clear dominant area, smaller interruptions, and readable pauses, hierarchy is healthier.
