# Shoujo A

## Identity

Relationship-driven, emotional-amplification shoujo manga.

Structural study reference: works such as *A Sign of Affection*.

The goal is not to imitate a specific artist. Extract the narrative logic.

## Narrative engine

**relationship state → opportunity → unspoken feeling → observable cue → perception shift → emotional landing**

External plot may remain small.

A tiny relationship shift is enough if it becomes visible.

## Attention is the page engine

Before selecting a close-up, decide:

> What is the viewpoint character noticing right now?

Then let visual hierarchy follow that attention.

If attention narrows to:
- eyes
- hands
- mouth
- scarf
- breath
- fingertips
- physical distance

reduce unrelated background information.

Background suppression should have a POV or emotional reason.

## What carries the page

Primary:
- gaze
- hands
- partial close-ups
- physical distance
- hesitation
- body orientation
- silence
- selective atmosphere
- page hierarchy contrast

Secondary:
- external plot complexity

## Hierarchy rule

Shoujo-A pages should usually show a **clear asymmetry of importance**.

Aim for:
- one dominant emotional panel
- one or two support panels
- one micro/insert/pause panel
- at least one clear hierarchy change

Hierarchy change may come from:
- a visibly larger panel
- a narrow vertical insert
- an open unframed or weakly framed panel
- a diagonal cut
- a border-breaking figure
- a strongly isolated local close-up

Do not reduce the page into evenly sparse rectangles.

## Panel tendency

Use more:
- asymmetry
- narrow emotional panels
- occasional diagonal panels
- white space
- character showcase panels
- local close-ups
- open panels

But every irregular panel needs a reason.

### Character showcase panel

Use when the viewpoint character:
- notices someone strongly
- re-evaluates them
- becomes newly conscious of them
- admires them

This is not a character sheet.

It is a perception event.

## Background strategy

Use realistic environment when orientation is needed.

During subjective emotional moments, surroundings may reduce to:
- white space
- sparse screentone
- reduced contours
- printed white flecks
- restrained atmosphere lines

Do not apply romantic effects everywhere.

## Local close-ups

Eyes, hands, lips, sleeve edges, scarves, breath, or almost-touching space should appear only when they carry emotional information.

Core rule:

> A local close-up is emotional magnification, not decorative detail.

## Chibi / super-deformed beat

Optional.

Use as a short pressure-release valve for:
- embarrassment
- panic
- awkwardness
- comic self-consciousness

Return to the main emotional register afterward.

Do not insert chibi randomly inside a serious intimate beat.

## Dialogue rhythm

Prefer:
- short lines
- unfinished thoughts
- pauses
- silence
- separated bubbles when hesitation matters

The page may slow down around one small interaction.

## Typical failure

### Pretty illustration collage
Add cause-and-effect and functional pause panels.

### Random irregular panels
Assign each one a clear emotional function.

### Too many close-ups
Restore a grounding shot.

### Atmosphere overload
Tie atmosphere to attention, not decoration.

### Generic romance
Ask:
- what exactly was noticed?
- what remains unsaid?
- which small gesture leaked the feeling?
- what changed in distance or perception?

### Too evenly sparse
Rebuild the page around one dominant emotional panel and at least one smaller interruption panel.

## Success test

Ask:
- Does the page show what the character notices?
- Does a small relationship shift become legible?
- Do close-ups and background reduction have emotional reasons?
- Does the strongest panel represent changed perception rather than generic beauty?
- Is the page asymmetrical enough to feel emotionally alive?

If yes, the mode is working.
